A small guide to good cuts.

  1. Collect an opening.

    Search the starter hooks, save your own, and favorite the ones you want to try. “Use in clip” sends a hook to the studio.

  2. Find the moment.

    Import a local video. Drag the timeline handles, scrub to your start and end, then use “Set at playhead” or type a timestamp such as 01:24.50. You can also enter seconds.

  3. Make the frame work.

    Pick vertical, square, landscape, or original. Adjust crop position to keep your subject visible. “Keep full video” adds black bars instead of cutting off edges.

  4. Export, then review.

    Add multiple cuts to the queue. Each becomes an H.264 MP4. Hook overlays show up for the first three seconds; attached hook text is also available as a text download. Import an SRT to preview captions and export a separate subtitle file retimed to your cut.

  5. Collect several cuts.

    Apply an export preset, save your selection with notes, or bookmark the next 15 seconds. Saved moments keep their own settings. Select up to 20 and queue them together. Search and filter finished clips in Exports.

  6. Keep your library.

    Hooks and video copies persist in this app’s .data folder. Export your hook library for a portable backup. Imported backups add new hooks and skip matching text.

No paid API or sign-in is needed. This version uses manual trim points and fixed cropping; it does not detect speakers or post to social accounts. Generate a local transcript, select a passage, and optionally burn captions into the MP4. Composition guides are approximate and are not exported.
